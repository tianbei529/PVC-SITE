## Why thickness alone doesn't tell the whole story

Two 5 mm sheets from different factories can behave completely differently because of **density**. PVC foam board is specified by two numbers:

- **Thickness** (mm) — how thick the sheet is
- **Density** (g/cm³) — how much material is packed into it

The same 1220 × 2440 mm sheet at 5 mm weighs about **8.9 kg at 0.60 g/cm³** but only **6.7 kg at 0.45 g/cm³**. The lighter board is cheaper and fine for a poster backing; the denser board holds screws and survives handling in a busy print shop.

### The simple formula to estimate sheet weight

> Weight (kg) = length (m) × width (m) × thickness (mm) × density (g/cm³)

Example: a 2.44 × 1.22 m sheet, 5 mm thick, at 0.55 g/cm³:

2.44 × 1.22 × 5 × 0.55 = **8.2 kg per sheet**

Use this to estimate container loading — and to check that a supplier's quotation is physically consistent.

## Thickness guidelines by application

### UV printing and screen printing

For direct UV printing, **3 mm is the industry standard**. It stays flat in the printer, feeds well and costs the least. Move to 5 mm when the print will be framed or mounted on standoffs. Always choose a **Celuka** sheet for printing — its hard, glossy skin gives sharper dot gain than free foam.

### Exhibition stands and shop displays

**5 mm** is the sweet spot: light enough for one person to carry, rigid enough for a 1.2 m panel not to bow. For double-sided printed walls, 8 mm with a matte surface avoids reflections under spotlights.

### Kitchen and bathroom cabinets

Moisture is why buyers switch from plywood to PVC foam board here. Use **10 – 12 mm at 0.55 – 0.65 g/cm³** for doors and 15 – 18 mm for shelves over 600 mm span. Free foam boards screw and route more cleanly than Celuka in these thicknesses.

### Construction and wall cladding

**8 – 15 mm** sheets with flame-retardant formulation are used for partitions and wall linings. If your market requires fire certification, ask for the **V-0 / B1 grade** — we produce it with the same tooling.

## How thickness is measured (and how to check a supplier)

Thickness tolerance for a quality PVC foam board is **± 0.1 to ± 0.2 mm**. When you receive samples:

1. Measure at **five points**: four corners 50 mm in from the edge, plus the centre
2. Any spread larger than 0.3 mm means uneven foaming — you will see it as waviness after printing
3. Check the sheet against a flat table: rock it by pressing one corner

We measure every batch with a digital micrometer and log the results; full TDS with tolerance data ships with every order.
