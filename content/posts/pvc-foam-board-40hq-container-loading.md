One of the most common questions we get on WhatsApp before a first order is: **"How many sheets can I fit in one 40HQ?"** It is a smart question — the answer decides your shipping cost per board, and it tells you the supplier actually knows production, not just trading. Here are the real numbers from our loading yard in Xinxiang, Henan.

## Quick answer by thickness

A standard **40HQ (40 ft high cube)** container takes a maximum payload of roughly **26,000 – 27,500 kg** depending on the shipping line. With 1220 × 2440 mm boards, packed on pallets, our typical loading looks like this:

| Thickness | Density | Weight per sheet | Sheets in 40HQ | Total weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 mm | 0.50 g/cm³ | ~4.5 kg | ~5,800 sheets | ~26,100 kg |
| 5 mm | 0.50 g/cm³ | ~7.4 kg | ~3,500 sheets | ~25,900 kg |
| 8 mm | 0.55 g/cm³ | ~13.1 kg | ~1,980 sheets | ~25,900 kg |
| 10 mm | 0.55 g/cm³ | ~16.4 kg | ~1,600 sheets | ~26,200 kg |
| 18 mm | 0.60 g/cm³ | ~32.2 kg | ~800 sheets | ~25,760 kg |

> These are payload-limited numbers. For thin boards (1.7 – 3 mm), the container can actually run out of **space** before it runs out of **weight**, so the real quantity is lower — ask us for the exact plan for your thickness.

## The formula, so you can check any supplier

Sheet weight is simple geometry:

```
Weight (kg) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (mm) × Density (g/cm³)
```

For a 1220 × 2440 mm board that works out to:

```
Weight (kg) = 2.98 × Thickness (mm) × Density
```

Example: a 5 mm sheet at 0.50 g/cm³ weighs 2.98 × 5 × 0.50 = **7.4 kg**. Divide the container payload (say 26,500 kg) by 7.4 and you get roughly **3,580 sheets**. Suppliers who quote "one container" without mentioning density are hiding a big variable — the same 5 mm board can weigh 20% more at 0.60 g/cm³ than at 0.50.

## Why pallets matter

Boards loaded loose (bare stacks) squeeze in maybe 8% more sheets, but arrive scuffed and with broken corners. We load on **wooden pallets with corner protection and strapping**:

- Each pallet stack is banded and wrapped, so forkifts can move full stacks at destination
- Boards stay flat — no edge chips from shifting during ocean transit
- Unloading at your warehouse takes hours instead of days

For signage and cabinet buyers, a damaged board is a lost board. Pallet loading costs a little space but saves real money on the other end.

## One warning about "more sheets per container"

Some suppliers advertise higher sheet counts by **dropping density** — a 5 mm board at 0.40 g/cm³ is cheaper and lighter, so more sheets fit. It looks like a better deal until the boards arrive soft, sag on the wall, and screw-holding fails on the CNC line. Density decides quality. If a quote seems unusually cheap per sheet, ask for the density in writing — we put it on every PI.
